## 1. Value, quality, low-risk, size, momentum and seasonality evidence in the Nordics (magnitudes, periods, decay, large vs small caps)

### Takeaway
Three signal families have Nordic-specific, peer-reviewed support: **12-1 momentum** (strong regardless of firm size), **low risk** (low idiosyncratic volatility or low beta, strong from 2005 to 2024 but muted in large caps), and **value**. Value pays mainly in small caps and works better with a quality screen: EBIT/EV-type earnings yields beat plain B/M, and Nordic small caps are "junky" unless filtered. The headline Magic Formula and F-score returns come from non-peer-reviewed theses that are sensitive to costs and survivorship. Calendar effects (January, turn-of-month, Sell-in-May) have largely decayed and should get little or no weight.

### Cited Findings

#### Value (B/M, E/P, EV/EBIT, composite value)
- Nordic equity market, 1993–2017. The value anomaly earned excess returns **only when small stocks were part of the portfolio**, while the momentum effect was "strong and significant, irrespective of size". Winner stocks that are also value stocks earned **1.66% per month**, and winner growth stocks earned about the same, so the negative value/momentum correlation is driven by growth stocks. This is the first Nordic test of the Fisher-Shah-Titman (2016) average-rank combination. Grobys & Huhta-Halkola, *Applied Economics* 51(26):2872–2884 (2019) [search excerpt] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3226899); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/tafapplec/v_3a51_3ay_3a2019_3ai_3a26_3ap_3a2872-2884.htm)
- Oslo Børs, 1996–2016 (CBS master thesis). The market, size and B/M premia were all **negative** over the test period. HML was slightly negative overall, "mainly because of the large negative HML factor in recent crises in the Norwegian stock market". A market + momentum + turnover (liquidity) model, or momentum + turnover alone, explained returns about as well as the alternatives. [search excerpt] — [CBS Research Portal](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/beae3b6c-9189-4e30-9325-35ac337875dd/)
- Finland, 1991–2013. All tested value strategies beat the market, but **EBIT/EV gave the highest risk-adjusted returns**. The Magic Formula beat the market but was not superior to the other value strategies, "might not be superior in a small market environment". Davydov, Tikkanen & Äijö, *Nordic Journal of Business* 65(3–4):38–54 (2016) [search excerpt] — [Lund thesis summarising it](https://lup.lub.lu.se/luur/download?func=downloadFile&recordOId=8932178&fileOId=8932179); [Vaasa self-archive](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/83ce8cde-14e6-48c9-98e4-7dd3b6fe7629/content)
- Europe region (global, not Nordic-specific). Value premiums "decrease with size", and momentum spreads also "decrease from smaller to bigger stocks". Fama & French (2012), *JFE* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.05.011)
- Implementation detail. B/P built with timely (current) prices instead of lagged prices earns alphas of **305–378 bp/yr** over the standard method (US 1950–2011, global 1983–2011). Asness & Frazzini (2013), *JPM* [third-party note quoting the abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2013.39.4.049); [note](https://github.com/bvirrion/one-quant-book/blob/f05c39450739a582d09a104161d9d9d10dd33c24/sources/strategies-1/06-value-quality-and-low-risk.md)
- Gross profitability (GP/A) has "roughly the same power as book-to-market", and controlling for profitability "dramatically increases the performance of value strategies" (US evidence). Novy-Marx (2013), *JFE* [third-party note quoting the abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.01.003)

#### Quality, Piotroski F-score, Magic Formula, multifactor screens
- A quality-minus-junk (QMJ) factor (profitability, growth, safety) "earns significant risk-adjusted returns in the United States and across 24 countries". Global evidence; AQR's country file includes Nordic columns (see Q5). Asness, Frazzini & Pedersen (2019) [third-party note quoting OpenAlex abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-018-9470-2)
- Nordic Magic Formula, 1 Jan 1998 – 1 Jan 2008 (SSE thesis, Persson & Selander 2009). The portfolio's Sharpe ratio exceeded MSCI Nordic and the S&P 500 **with and without transaction costs**, and costs lowered CAGR to **11.98%**. [search excerpt] — [SSE thesis](https://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=769)
- A literature review (Poznań *REF*) reports two Nordic results. In Finland, Sweden, Norway and Denmark over **2000–2011**, the Magic Formula earned a net (after taxes and costs) average annual return of **29.4% vs 7.6%** for the FTSE Nordic Value index. On **Oslo Børs, May 2003–May 2022**, a monthly alpha of **0.5%** and CAGR outperformance of **8.02 pp** over OSEAX. The review concludes that effectiveness is "contingent on transaction costs (frequently eroding alpha), firm size, market conditions and metric modifications". [search excerpt; original authors of the 2000–2011 study not identified] — [Poznań review](https://www.journals.ue.poznan.pl/REF/article/view/2790)
- The same Oslo Magic Formula thesis (2003–2022, four-factor model) concludes that "real-world transaction costs diminish its effectiveness" and that "achieving risk-adjusted excess returns may not be feasible under actual market conditions". [search excerpt] — [thesis on Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/837654656/Master-Thesis)
- Denmark, Finland and Norway, **2012–2021**, compared with an earlier Swedish study (DiVA thesis). Magic Formula and F-score portfolios beat market indexes in most years. Both performed **best in Norway**. F-score had the higher accumulated return. Statistically significant differences between the strategies appeared only in Norway and Denmark. [search excerpt] — [DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1563677/FULLTEXT01.pdf)

#### Low volatility / betting against beta
- Nordic markets, **Feb 2005 – Mar 2024**, with 36/24/12-month risk windows. Low-risk portfolios beat high-risk portfolios in raw and risk-adjusted terms. **Idiosyncratic-volatility sorts are strongest and beta sorts weakest.** In **large caps the spreads are muted**, and only IVOL sorts remain significant. Results are robust for holding periods **up to 12 months**. Grobys, Hartikainen & Äijö, *Applied Economics* (online 30 Apr 2025) [search excerpt] — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00036846.2025.2497563); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391341775_Low-risk_anomalies_evidence_from_the_Nordic_equity_markets)
- "On a cross-country basis in the Nordic markets", BAB earns an average yearly alpha of **12.2%** with Sharpe **0.75**, and betting-against-volatility earns **5.1%** with Sharpe **0.59**. [search excerpt; **attribution uncertain**, probably the CBS student project rather than the peer-reviewed paper above] — [CBS project page](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/the-low-risk-anomaly-in-the-nordic-equity-markets-high-risk-high-/)
- Finland (OMX Helsinki, Vaasa thesis). Low-beta stocks had particularly strong returns, and BAB "performed convincingly with positive excess returns". [search excerpt] — [Vaasa thesis](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/bitstreams/15ea676a-27a6-4dd9-a30a-7ae9d10191b3/download)
- Nordic equities, **Jan 1999 – Sep 2022** (*Applied Economics*, 2024). Low-volatility and momentum both persist as pure strategies. All long-only combinations beat the market risk-adjusted, and the momentum-first double screen had the best Sharpe. No long-short combination beat **pure momentum** after Fama-French five-factor adjustment: momentum enhances low-vol, not the reverse. [search excerpt] — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00036846.2024.2337806)
- Global context. High-IVOL stocks have low future returns "around the world": across **23 developed markets** the extreme-quintile spread is **−1.31%/month** after world market, size and value controls. Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang (2009), *JFE* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2007.12.005)
- Global context. High beta is associated with low alpha in US equities and **20 international equity markets**, and BAB earns significant positive risk-adjusted returns. Frazzini & Pedersen (2014), *JFE* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.10.005)

#### Size and illiquidity
- Oslo Børs, 1980–2016. The CAPM "does not seem able to price size portfolios or liquidity portfolios, indicating a size effect in the Norwegian market related to liquidity". Ødegaard, *Empirics of the OSE: Asset pricing results 1980–2016* (UiS WP 2017/02) [search excerpt; a 1980–2020 edition also exists] — [UiS PDF](http://www6.uis.no/ansatt/odegaard/uis_wps_econ_fin/uis_wps_2017_02_odegaard_asset_pricing.pdf); [2021 edition](https://ba-odegaard.no/wps/empirics_ose_asset_pricing/empirics_ose_asset_pricing_2021_03.pdf)
- Nordic markets (Jyväskylä thesis). SMB is "predominantly negative". Size is "largely negatively correlated to quality", so Nordic small caps are "largely composed of low-quality or 'junky' stocks". Controlling for QMJ improves SMB only **marginally** (mainly Sweden and Finland). [search excerpt] — [JYX record](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_104086)
- Global. Size-premium critiques (weak, concentrated in microcaps and January, weak internationally) "disappear when controlling for the quality, or its inverse, junk". A size premium then appears that is stable, "not concentrated in microcaps", and holds in **24 international equity markets**. Asness, Frazzini, Israel, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2018), *JFE* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.05.006)

#### Momentum (12-1 and variants)
- Oslo Børs (earlier studies cited in a CBS thesis; their sample periods not given in the excerpt). Sixteen momentum strategies earned significant returns of **1.19%–2.43% per month**. A 12-month formation / 3-month holding strategy earned **1.31%/month**, or **1.49%/month** with a one-week lag. [search excerpt] — [CBS thesis PDF](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/58452773/kristoffer_absalonsen_og_alexander_vas.pdf)
- Ødegaard's Norwegian momentum factor PR1YR is a Carhart (1997)-style factor computed on Norwegian data. Per a search excerpt it ranks on returns over the previous eleven months and is the top-30% minus bottom-30% return. [search excerpt] — [Ødegaard OSE asset-pricing data page](http://finance.bi.no/~bernt/financial_data/ose_asset_pricing_data/index.html); factor description confirmed in [Quandl metadata copy](https://github.com/jimmoores/quandl4j/blob/7331289a57980dfd891eadf674948f355e143f1d/core/src/test/resources/com/jimmoores/quandl/tests/testresults/MetaData46.json)
- Twelve European countries, **1980–1995**. Past winners beat past losers by **more than 1%/month** risk-adjusted. Continuation appears "in all twelve sample countries", lasts about **one year**, and is negatively related to firm size "but is not limited to small firms". Rouwenhorst (1998), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.95722)
- Momentum has the highest Sharpe ratio among market, value and size, but also the worst crashes. Its risk is predictable, and **scaling by recent volatility "virtually eliminates crashes and nearly doubles the Sharpe ratio"** (US). Barroso & Santa-Clara (2015), *JFE* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2014.11.010)
- Value and momentum premia are consistent across eight markets and asset classes, and value and momentum are **negatively correlated**, which favours combining them. Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12021)

#### Seasonality (January, turn-of-month, Sell-in-May, same-month)
- Buying stocks on their historical **same-calendar-month** returns earns **13% per year**, with similar seasonalities in anomalies and in **international stock market indices**. Keloharju, Linnainmaa & Nyberg (2016), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12398)
- "Across 37 countries stock market returns are significantly higher in the period November-April than in May-October" (Halloween / Sell-in-May). Bouman & Jacobsen (2002), *AER* [abstract excerpt via third-party note] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/000282802762024683); [note](https://github.com/EPOCHDevs/ClaudeCodeResearch/blob/b10c9b7550247128cc53ca0e9efd6f5ac515ba4c/reference_notes/market-seasonality-effect-in-world-equity-indexes/index.md)
- Decay evidence, all **[third-party note]** from one GitHub research file and not independently checked: the turn-of-month effect appears in **31 of 35 countries** (McConnell & Xu 2008, *FAJ*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v64.n2.11)) but "disappears entirely after 2001" (Han, Han & Tian 2025, *FRL*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2024.106461)). Calendar anomalies "largely disappear" from the early 1990s, with Sell-in-May a partial exception internationally (Zakamulin 2026, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.najef.2026.102653)). After controlling for fund flows, the January and Sell-in-May effects "become insignificant" (Wagner, Lee & Margaritis 2022, *JBF*, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2022.106623)) — [note](https://github.com/luke-cramer/ai-trading/blob/main/research/seasonality-verify.md)

### Inferences
- **Value: weight it, but only alongside quality, mainly in small and mid caps, over 6–36 months.** Nordic value worked only with small stocks included (Grobys), and Nordic small caps are junky (JYX thesis). Value should therefore be an earnings/cash-flow yield composite (EBIT/EV, E/P, FCF yield, with B/M as a minor input) filtered by quality (profitability, F-score, accruals), not raw B/M. EBIT/EV beating the Magic Formula in Finland points the same way.
- **Momentum (12-1): substantial weight, 3–12 month holding, all size segments.** It is the most consistently documented Nordic factor, including in large caps. Volatility-scaling (Barroso & Santa-Clara) is the cheapest crash protection a long-only investor can use. Because value and momentum are negatively correlated, use momentum as a co-signal or tie-breaker for value names rather than a separate sleeve.
- **Low risk: use as a filter, holding up to 12 months.** Exclude or penalise the top IVOL quintile instead of running a leveraged BAB, which a Nordnet retail account cannot replicate cheaply. In large caps only IVOL works, so beta sorts there deserve little weight.
- **Size alone gets no weight.** The Nordic SMB evidence is negative or ambiguous, and the OSE size effect is largely an illiquidity effect. Small caps should be a habitat where value and quality work, not a bet in themselves.
- **Treat thesis-level Magic Formula and F-score magnitudes as upper bounds.** The 29.4% net and +8 pp/yr figures come from non-peer-reviewed theses with unclear survivorship and point-in-time handling. A realistic expectation after decay and costs is far smaller (see Q4).
- **Seasonality: weight about zero.** At most, log it as a diagnostic, because the decay and flow-based explanations dominate recent evidence.
- **Expect Oslo results to differ from Stockholm's.** HML on OSE was negative in crises (CBS thesis) and OSE's sector mix differs (energy, shipping, seafood). Factor scores should therefore be computed within sector, or sector-neutralised, before cross-sectional ranking. This is an inference, not tested in the sources found.

### Gaps
- Exact Ødegaard OSE factor premia (mean monthly SMB, HML, PR1YR, LIQ and t-stats) could not be extracted: ba-odegaard.no and uis.no were blocked.
- No source was found that separately quantifies FCF yield, dividend yield, shareholder yield, ROIC, accruals or earnings stability for the Nordics.
- No Nasdaq Stockholm–specific peer-reviewed multifactor test was found. Swedish Magic Formula and F-score results are only referenced indirectly (DiVA thesis).
- No Nordic-specific seasonality study was verified. Ødegaard's descriptive OSE papers reportedly cover calendar effects but could not be opened.
- Rouwenhorst's twelve countries: recalled but unverified that they include Denmark, Norway and Sweden.
- No evidence was found splitting results for First North or Euronext Growth Oslo stocks specifically.
- The authorship of the "BAB alpha 12.2%" figure is uncertain.

## 2. Medium-term event signals (1–12 months): PEAD, analysts, buybacks, dividends, equity issues, index changes, short interest, ownership changes, media

### Takeaway
Nordic event evidence comes mostly from master theses with mixed results. Buybacks show the most consistent positive post-announcement drift in Sweden. PEAD is inconsistent across Swedish studies and appears small on Oslo Børs. Analyst recommendations give small, short-lived returns that are hard to capture. On Oslo Børs, equity issues (rights issues and private placements) show no reliable long-run drift, unlike the US underperformance. Short-interest and ownership data are freely available, but Nordic return-prediction evidence is thin. These signals suit overlays and tie-breakers, not core weights.

### Cited Findings

#### Post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD)
- Sweden, Jan 2001 – Jul 2006 (SSE thesis): PEAD "works well for large firms but not for small firms". [search excerpt] — [SSE thesis](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=297)
- Sweden (Uppsala thesis): no PEAD for firms with **good corporate governance**; firms with bad governance did drift. [search excerpt] — [DiVA](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1452207/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- A Swedish study: long top-SUE quintile / short bottom-SUE quintile gave a significant annual abnormal return of **18.65%** with a **60-day** holding period. [search excerpt; **exact document uncertain**, among SSE theses] — [SSE thesis A](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=297); [SSE thesis B](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=2927)
- First study of Swedish market reactions to quarterly accounting information (PEAD and momentum), by Setterberg. [search excerpt; no magnitudes captured] — [SSE working paper](https://staffstream.hhs.se/public/streamdocument.ashx?dl=01014_001)
- Oslo Børs (CBS thesis). The market is "efficient to a large extent". Significant abnormal returns occur **on the announcement day and the day after** quarterly reports, which implies little drift to harvest at monthly horizons. [search excerpt] — [CBS portal](https://research.cbs.dk/da/studentProjects/ef598439-563b-45f1-9c6d-3853655cdbc9/)
- Europe, 2018 (Aalto thesis). Significant PEAD, especially after **negative** earnings news, and the magnitude is **inversely related to firm size**. [search excerpt] — [Aaltodoc](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/38a25c67-8911-495c-bef4-8a2645ae7ceb)
- International. An exogenous improvement in non-US firms' financial-reporting quality (an information shock; the authors' setting is IFRS-related) made **PEAD diminish**, more so where institutional ownership was higher and arbitrage constraints lower. This supports a limited-attention mispricing explanation. Hung, Li & Wang (2015), *RFS* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhu092)
- US. Across 97 anomalies, returns are **50% higher on corporate news days and six times higher on earnings-announcement days**, consistent with biased expectations being corrected when news arrives. Engelberg, McLean & Pontiff (2018), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12718)

#### Analyst recommendations and revisions
- Stockholm, **2010–2014**, 4,566 recommendations: average 3-day event-window abnormal return **1.58%**, and 6-month holding-period abnormal return **2.54%**. [search excerpt] — [SSE thesis](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=3018)
- A further SSE thesis found absolute abnormal returns above 1% for both upgrades and downgrades. [search excerpt] — [SSE thesis](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=1307)
- A Nordic-countries study (Vaasa thesis) concluded "it is not possible to earn abnormal returns by following analysts' recommendations". [search excerpt] — [Osuva](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/items/2b460df5-9cef-48f4-9763-3e89a45771e4)
- Stockholm, 1997–2006 broker-level data: "recommending brokers execute profitable trades around positive recommendation revision dates", which suggests the information reaches the broker's flow before the public. Anderson & Martinez [search excerpt] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2019072)

#### Share buybacks
- Stockholm, **2005–2013** repurchase announcements: long-run abnormal returns of **14.34% (large), 20.13% (medium) and 6.61% (small)** firms. [search excerpt; horizon not captured] — [DiVA thesis](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1222662/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- Stockholm, **2000–2009**: **6%–7%** positive abnormal performance in the 12 months after initiation, positively related to the fraction of shares bought back. Two-day announcement abnormal return is about **2%**. A study of **458** Swedish open-market repurchase announcements suggests persistent underreaction. [search excerpt; the individual studies behind each number are not cleanly separated] — [SSE bachelor thesis](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=6372); [Price impact of OMRs](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228315600_The_Price_Impact_of_Open_Market_Share_Repurchases)
- **Correction:** one search summary presented "12.1% four-year abnormal return, 45.3% for value stocks" as Swedish. Those figures are actually **US 1980–1990** (Ikenberry, Lakonishok & Vermaelen 1995), where glamour stocks showed no drift. [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(95)00826-Z)
- International buyback evidence: Manconi, Peyer & Vermaelen, "Evidence from buybacks around the world", *JFQA* 54. Title verified; content not retrieved. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109018000984)

#### Equity issues: rights issues and private placements (frequent on Oslo Børs)
- Oslo Børs rights issues (1980s–90s sample). Seasoned public offerings there take place almost exclusively through standby-underwritten rights. The market reaction to **uninsured rights offers is significantly positive**, while **standby-underwritten issues get the least favourable reaction**, consistent with asymmetric information. Bøhren, Eckbo & Michalsen (1997), *JFE* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-405X(97)00030-5)
- Oslo Børs private placements: average announcement reaction is significantly positive, and long-run abnormal returns are "indistinguishable from zero". [search excerpt; **source paper not identified**, surfaced alongside Øyvind Norli's BI profile] — [BI profile](https://www.bi.no/en/About-bi/employees/department-of-finance/oyvind-norli/)
- US contrast: private placements have positive announcement effects but **negative post-announcement performance**, read as investor over-optimism. Hertzel, Lemmon, Linck & Rees (2002), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1540-6261.00507). A related summary cites a −23.8% three-year matched-firm BHAR [search excerpt] — [PlacementTracker sample](https://www.placementtracker.com/samplereports/LongRunPerformance.pdf)

#### Dividend initiations and omissions
- US: firms initiating dividends have positive earnings changes before and after, and omitters have negative ones. Investors treat initiations and omissions as managers' earnings forecasts. Healy & Palepu (1988), *JFE* 21(2) [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(88)90059-1); [abstract mirror](https://github.com/danielyang1009/light-speed-engine/blob/6bd63c47eb1a3582377c52033966116c18575953/JFE/Volume%2021/Volume%2021%20-%20Issue%202.md)
- No Nordic-specific dividend initiation or cut return study was found (see Gaps).

#### Short interest
- EU short-sale disclosure regime. Positions **accumulate just below the public disclosure threshold**, and positions held by these secretive short sellers are followed by **stronger negative returns**, suggesting superior information. Jank, Roling & Smajlbegovic (2021), *JFE* 139(1):209–233 [search excerpt; I believe the dataset is from a single regulator, likely German, which is not verified] — [EUR PDF](https://pure.eur.nl/files/56168354/1_s2.0_S0304405X20302075_main.pdf)
- Data availability: Sweden's FI publishes significant net short positions "on a daily basis, normally shortly after 15:30" ([finsyn/blankningsregistret-js README quoting FI](https://github.com/finsyn/blankningsregistret-js)). Norway's Finanstilsynet runs a short-selling register with an open JSON API at `ssr.finanstilsynet.no/api/v2` ([samuelsmb/SSR code](https://github.com/samuelsmb/SSR); [bjelle-ai concept doc](https://github.com/kennyng90/bjelle-ai/blob/6543f81ccc3dc04771262c18927715390d9d882a/docs/CONCEPT.md)).

#### Institutional and foreign ownership changes
- Sweden's pension reform raised pension-fund equity ownership. **Firm valuation improves when public pension funds and large independent private pension funds increase their holdings.** Giannetti & Laeven (2009), *RFS* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhn091)
- Swedish shareholdings (Merton investor recognition): returns are positively related to the "shadow cost of incomplete information", and the **size of the shareholder base is negatively related to returns** controlling for size and idiosyncratic risk. Zero-cost portfolios formed on these yield "substantial trading profits", only modestly explained by the four-factor model. Bodnaruk & Östberg (2009), *JFE* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2008.01.006)

#### Media coverage and news sentiment
- No Nordic return-predictability study using media coverage or news sentiment was found or verified. The relevant general evidence is US-only (Engelberg-McLean-Pontiff above).

### Inferences
- **Buybacks: positive overlay, 6–12 months.** Use announcements or executed volumes as a modest positive tilt, larger when the stock is also cheap on the value composite (consistent with the US value/glamour asymmetry). Börsdata Pro+ exposes buyback holdings (see Q5).
- **PEAD: low weight, 1–3 months, only as a tie-breaker.** Nordic evidence conflicts (one Swedish study finds drift only in large firms, another 18.65%/yr, Oslo is nearly efficient). Reporting-quality shocks reduced PEAD internationally. If used, combine an earnings surprise (vs consensus, or a seasonal random walk) with the announcement-window return and decay its weight to zero by around 60 trading days.
- **Analyst changes: very low weight.** The retail investor sees them last (Anderson & Martinez), the effect is mostly within days (out of scope), and the 6-month excess of about 2.5% in one Swedish sample is likely below costs plus noise.
- **Equity issues on Oslo Børs: risk flag, not a return signal.** No reliable Norwegian long-run drift was found. Discounted private placements create short-term supply overhangs (another researcher's horizon). The US negative drift supports penalising frequent issuers in the quality score (net share issuance), a standard quality component.
- **Short interest: a free negative flag.** Treat stocks with large or rising disclosed net short positions in the FI or Finanstilsynet registers as a negative tilt. Because informed shorts may hide below 0.5%, absence of disclosure is not a clean bill of health.
- **Ownership: a small premium for neglect.** The Swedish investor-recognition result implies a small premium for neglected stocks with narrow shareholder bases. That is compensation for under-diversification and illiquidity, not free alpha.

### Gaps
- No Nordic index-inclusion study (OBX, OMXS30, OMXC25, OMXH25) was found or verified.
- No Nordic study was found on dividend initiations, cuts or omissions.
- No Nordic study links media coverage or news sentiment to returns. Norwegian newspaper-topic work (Thorsrud et al.) was not retrieved.
- No Nordic study links short interest to returns, and none links quarterly changes in institutional or foreign ownership to subsequent returns.
- The 2000–2009 Swedish buyback numbers and the Oslo private-placement study could not be tied to specific authors because sources were blocked.

## 3. Market efficiency and the hypothesis that smaller, lower-volume Scandinavian markets with less advanced traders should be fruitful

### Takeaway
The hypothesis is **partly supported**, but in a narrower form than stated. Anomalies outside the US have *not* decayed after publication, and Nordic evidence shows strong momentum, low-risk and small-cap value effects plus a neglected-stock premium in Sweden. However, Nordic "less advanced traders", meaning retail households, are the ones who **lose** (Sweden about −8.5%/yr, Norwegian investors negative in familiar stocks, Finnish households beaten by foreigners). Swedish active funds show roughly zero net alpha. Small-firm trading costs on Oslo Børs have not fallen. A realistic edge for an individual comes from patience, low turnover and the freedom to hold small, neglected but high-quality names. Trading against the crowd at high frequency is not the source.

### Cited Findings

#### Are anomalies more persistent outside the US?
- Across **241 anomalies in 39 stock markets** (more than two million anomaly country-months), "the United States is the only country with a reliable post-publication decline in long/short returns". The authors suggest barriers to arbitrage may segment markets and that anomalies "tend to represent mispricing rather than data mining". Jacobs & Müller (2020), *JFE* 135(1):213–230 [search excerpt plus a verbatim quote in a third-party note] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.06.004); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2816490)
- Most factors "work out-of-sample in a new large data set covering 93 countries". Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13249)

#### Information diffusion, analyst coverage and investor recognition (the "neglect" channel)
- Past the very smallest stocks, momentum profitability "declines sharply with firm size". Holding size fixed, "momentum strategies work better among stocks with **low analyst coverage**", especially past losers, so bad news travels slowly (US). Hong, Lim & Stein (2000), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00206)
- Sweden: a smaller shareholder base predicts higher returns, and investor-recognition portfolios earn substantial profits (Bodnaruk & Östberg 2009, above) [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2008.01.006)
- In the Nordics, value works only with small stocks included, while momentum works across sizes (Grobys & Huhta-Halkola 2019, Q1) [search excerpt] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3226899)

#### Who wins and who loses among Nordic investors
- Finland (shareholder register). **Foreign investors are momentum traders and outperform domestic households**, households are contrarian, and the households' inferior performance persists after adjusting for their contrarian style. Grinblatt & Keloharju (2000), *JFE* [verified abstract and search excerpt] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-405X(99)00044-6)
- Finland. Investors overweight nearby firms, firms that communicate in their native tongue and firms whose CEO shares their culture. The effect is weaker among the most investment-savvy institutions. Grinblatt & Keloharju (2001), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00355)
- Finland. **High-IQ investors** show superior market timing, stock-picking and trade execution, and less disposition effect. Grinblatt, Keloharju & Linnainmaa (2012), *JFE* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.05.016)
- Finland. Individuals' apparent losses around earnings announcements, poor post-trade returns, disposition effect and contrarianism "can be explained in large part by investors' use of **limit orders**". Linnainmaa (2010), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01576.x)
- Norway, all individual investors' transactions over 10 years. Individuals hold 11% of their portfolios in stocks from their own two-digit industry, and **all estimates of abnormal returns on these are negative**, many significantly so. The likely explanation is overconfidence. Døskeland & Hvide (2011), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2011.01658.x)
- Sweden, contrasting result. Familiarity-based holdings are "information driven" and earn **higher** returns than hedging would. Massa & Simonov (2006), *RFS* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhj013)
- Sweden, Internet discount broker, **May 1999 – Mar 2002**: 16,831 investors and 324,736 trades. The median investor held **2.33 stocks**. Investors **underperformed the market by about 8.5% per year, largely due to trading costs**, and high-stake, concentrated, heavy traders did worst. Anderson, "All Guts, No Glory" (2007) and "Trading and Under-Diversification", *Review of Finance* (2013) [search excerpt] — [ResearchGate 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228265693_Trading_and_Under-Diversification); [ResearchGate 2007](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227977262_All_Guts_No_Glory_Trading_and_Diversification_among_Online_Investors)
- Sweden. Households shift from growth to value as they age and their balance sheets improve. The patterns are "remarkably consistent" with risk-based explanations of the value premium. Betermier, Calvet & Sodini (2017), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12473)

#### Professional managers in the "inefficient" market
- Swedish active equity funds, **1999–2009**: 4-factor alpha of **+0.9%/yr before expenses and −0.5%/yr after**. There is "practically no persistence": rankings converge to the mean in about two years, and "practically no evidence of true management skill". Extended to **1993–2013**, funds outperformed in 1993–2001 but had gross and net excess returns of **−0.18% and −1.47%/yr in 2002–2013**. Flam & Vestman [search excerpt] — [SSRN 2414137](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2414137); [SSRN 3082073](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3082073)

#### Liquidity and trading costs
- Oslo Børs, **1980–2007**. Proportional (relative) spread costs "**have not decreased over the sample period for small firms**". Liquidity worsens when the economy slows, most so for small firms, and investor participation co-moves with liquidity, especially for the smallest firms. Næs, Skjeltorp & Ødegaard, Norges Bank WP 2008/9 [search excerpt] — [WP PDF](https://ba-odegaard.no/wps/liquidity_ose/Norges_Bank_Working_Paper_2008_9.pdf). The published version shows a strong link between stock-market liquidity and the business cycle and a "flight to quality" in downturns; *JF* (2011) [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01628.x)
- The stocks with the highest momentum returns have the highest trading costs, making momentum profits largely "illusory" (US). Lesmond, Schill & Zhou (2004), *JFE* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-405X(03)00206-X)
- "Most anomalies with less than **50% turnover per month** generate significant net spreads when designed to mitigate transaction costs; few with higher turnover do." A **buy/hold spread** (stricter rules to enter than to stay) is the most effective cost-mitigation technique. Size, value and profitability have the greatest capacity. Novy-Marx & Velikov (2016), *RFS* [verified abstract] — [abstract mirror](https://github.com/danielyang1009/light-speed-engine/blob/6bd63c47eb1a3582377c52033966116c18575953/RFS/Volume%2029/Volume%2029%20-%20Issue%201.md)
- Nordnet Sweden commission tiers, from an undated third-party code snapshot (**must be checked against nordnet.se**; the site was blocked here). **Mini** 0.25%, minimum 1 SEK; **Liten** 0.15%, minimum 39 SEK; **Mellan** 0.069%, minimum 69 SEK; **Fast** 99 SEK flat (0%). The same code applies a separate currency-conversion cost when currencies differ. [third-party note] — [kallegrens/rebalance courtage.py](https://github.com/kallegrens/rebalance/blob/main/rebalance/courtage.py)
- Practitioner evidence (Börsdata or Börslabbet backtests, Investtech, Redeye, Inderes, Nordic quant funds): **none could be retrieved** (borslabbet.se and investtech.com blocked, search budget exhausted). Börsdata's own API documentation says nothing about survivorship (delisted firms) or point-in-time data integrity, which matters for any backtest built on it. — [Börsdata API README](https://github.com/Borsdata-Sweden/API); [Börsdata API wiki](https://github.com/Borsdata-Sweden/API/wiki)

### Inferences
- **Balanced verdict.** The structural ingredients the user describes are real: anomalies persist outside the US, momentum works better where coverage is thin, and neglected Swedish stocks earn a premium. But the people "less advanced" than the market in Nordic data are mostly retail households, who systematically lose through turnover, concentration and familiarity bias. The side that wins (foreign and institutional investors, high-IQ individuals) behaves like a diversified, momentum-consistent, low-turnover investor. The app should therefore exploit slow information diffusion with **patience**, not with frequent trading.
- **Where a retail edge plausibly exists:**
  - Small and mid caps that institutions are too big or too constrained to hold, but only when screened for quality.
  - Holding periods of 6–24 months, where per-trade costs are amortised.
  - Avoiding the behavioural mistakes documented in the Nordic register studies: concentration, overtrading, and buying familiar or "story" stocks.
- **Cost arithmetic (inference from the unverified Nordnet tiers).** A 100,000 SEK account split into 20 positions of 5,000 SEK on the Mini tier pays about 12.5 SEK, 0.25%, per side, or roughly 0.5% round trip before spread. Annual rebalancing with about 50% turnover costs roughly 0.25%/yr in commission. Monthly momentum rebalancing with about 30–50% monthly turnover could cost 2–3%/yr in commission alone, before small-cap spreads. That is enough to wipe out much of a momentum premium in illiquid names, consistent with Lesmond et al. and Novy-Marx & Velikov. Use buy/hold bands, rank-based hysteresis, and a minimum trade size.
- **Liquidity risk is the price of the small-cap habitat.** The NSO evidence implies small-cap drawdowns and spread widening cluster in recessions, so position limits should scale with turnover (for example, 1–5% of median daily value traded per position) and the app should not assume fills at mid-price.
- **"Nordic professionals haven't arbitraged it away" is weak support.** Swedish active funds show roughly zero net alpha, so even well-resourced local professionals do not reliably extract an inefficiency premium net of fees. A retail strategy's main advantage is lower costs and no benchmark constraints, not better information.

### Gaps
- Official current Nordnet commission tiers for Norwegian, Danish and Finnish shares, the Norwegian (NOK) class minimums, and Nordnet's currency-exchange fee could not be verified (nordnet.se and nordnet.no blocked).
- No current numbers were found for quoted or effective bid-ask spreads on Oslo Børs small caps, Euronext Growth Oslo or First North. Ødegaard's "The (implicit) cost of equity trading at the OSE" (UiS WP 2009/17, [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hhs/stavef/2009_017.html)) and "The Liquidity of the OSE" (2017, [PDF](https://ba-odegaard.no/uis_wps_econ_fin/uis_wps_2017_01_odegaard_liquidity.pdf)) were blocked.
- No study of the MiFID II analyst-coverage drop for Nordic small caps or of commissioned research (Redeye, Inderes, Edison) was retrieved. No result was obtained for the NHH thesis on trend-chasing bias in Norwegian equity research reports ([listed in an NHH theses scrape](https://github.com/bredeespelid/Master_NHH)).
- No practitioner backtest (Börslabbet, Investtech, Inderes or Redeye track records, Nordic quant funds) could be read or evaluated, so these remain unassessed. Any such claims should be treated as in-sample and potentially survivorship-biased until replicated.
- No post-2010 Norwegian retail broker-data study was found, apart from the register-based Døskeland-Hvide study.

## 4. Combining signals, out-of-sample decay, multiple testing and expected degradation

### Takeaway
Plan for heavy shrinkage. In the US, published anomalies earn about **26% less out-of-sample and 58% less after publication**, about 65% of 452 anomalies fail a value-weighted replication, and net of costs and the modern trading era the average anomaly earns roughly **4 bp/month**. Outside the US, post-publication decay has *not* been reliable, and factor *themes* (value, momentum, quality, low risk) replicate well under Bayesian shrinkage. The app should therefore combine a **few theme-level composites** with conservative priors, demand t > 3 for any new home-grown signal, and treat its own logged predictions as the real out-of-sample test.

### Cited Findings

#### Post-publication decay and replication
- **97 predictors**: "Portfolio returns are **26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower post-publication** … We estimate a **32% (58%–26%)** lower return from publication-informed trading." McLean & Pontiff (2016), *JF* 71(1):5–32 [verified abstract via verbatim copies] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365); [verbatim copy](https://github.com/bvirrion/one-quant-book/blob/f05c39450739a582d09a104161d9d9d10dd33c24/sources/research/13-half-life-decay-and-stability.md)
- With microcaps mitigated through NYSE breakpoints and value-weighted returns, "**65% of the 452 anomalies** … cannot clear the single test hurdle of the absolute t-value of 1.96". Even replicated anomalies have "much smaller" magnitudes than originally reported. Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020), *RFS* [Crossref abstract quoted in third-party note] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhy131); [note](https://github.com/bvirrion/one-quant-book/blob/f05c39450739a582d09a104161d9d9d10dd33c24/sources/research/13-half-life-decay-and-stability.md)
- A Bayesian replication model finds that most factors "(i) can be replicated; (ii) can be clustered into **13 themes** … (iii) work out-of-sample in a new large data set covering **93 countries**; and (iv) have evidence that is strengthened (not weakened) by the large number of observed factors". Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen (2023), *JF* [verified abstract] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13249); [NBER abstract JSON](https://github.com/ledwindra/nber/blob/f66bee61eab0a798a92ec8f8af3231e5e6e47e5b/data/nber/28432.json)
- Outside the US, none of 38 markets shows a reliable post-publication decline (Jacobs & Müller 2020) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.06.004)
- Returns of a portfolio of prominent anomalies "approximately halved after decimalization", attributed to growth in hedge-fund AUM, short interest and turnover. Chordia, Subrahmanyam & Tong (2014), *JAE* [Crossref abstract quoted in third-party note] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2014.06.001)
- A third-party recomputation on Open Source Asset Pricing data reproduces the McLean-Pontiff pattern: across 212 signals, mean in-sample **0.690%/month** falls to **0.324%/month** after publication (ratio 0.47). Seasonality signals decay slightly worse than average (ratio 0.439). [third-party note, not peer-reviewed] — [luke-cramer/ai-trading](https://github.com/luke-cramer/ai-trading/blob/main/research/seasonality-verify.md)

#### Multiple testing
- "A newly discovered factor needs to clear a much higher hurdle, with a **t-ratio greater than 3.0** … most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false." Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016), *RFS* [verified abstract, NBER WP 20592] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv059); [NBER abstract JSON](https://github.com/ledwindra/nber/blob/f66bee61eab0a798a92ec8f8af3231e5e6e47e5b/data/nber/20592.json)

#### Costs and the modern trading era
- Across **204 anomalies**, accounting for effective bid-ask spreads, post-publication effects and "the modern era of trading technology that began in the early 2000s", "the average anomaly's expected return is a measly **4 bps per month**. The strongest anomalies net, at best, **10 bps** after controlling for data mining". Note: a third-party summary puts gross in-sample returns at about 68 bp/month. This is US evidence. Chen & Velikov (2023), *JFQA* [abstract quoted verbatim in third-party notes] — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109022000874); [verbatim copy](https://github.com/viki-m13/bonds/blob/5f6cb8ffb14bbddf84e156a507cd3e5b37e77957/dca/research/strategies/ascent/methods_survey_claims.json)
- Anomalies with under 50% monthly turnover tend to survive costs. Size, value and profitability have the most capacity (Novy-Marx & Velikov 2016, Q3) — [abstract mirror](https://github.com/danielyang1009/light-speed-engine/blob/6bd63c47eb1a3582377c52033966116c18575953/RFS/Volume%2029/Volume%2029%20-%20Issue%201.md)

#### Combining signals (Nordic and global)
- Value and momentum are negatively correlated within and across asset classes (Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen 2013) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12021)
- In the Nordics, an average-rank value and momentum combination was tested. The value/momentum negative correlation is driven by growth stocks, and winner-value stocks earn 1.66%/month (Grobys & Huhta-Halkola 2019) — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3226899)
- Nordic low-vol plus momentum, 1999–2022: long-only combinations beat the market risk-adjusted, and momentum-first double screening had the best Sharpe, but no long-short combination beat pure momentum after five-factor adjustment ([T&F 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00036846.2024.2337806))
- Controlling for profitability "dramatically" improves value (Novy-Marx 2013), and controlling for junk revives size (Asness et al. 2018) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.01.003); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.05.006)
- Volatility-managed momentum nearly doubles momentum's Sharpe ratio (Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2014.11.010)

### Inferences
- **Expected degradation to plan for in the formula.** For a factor documented in Nordic in-sample studies, assume the forward gross premium is about 40–60% of the published number (McLean-Pontiff, HXZ). The Jacobs-Müller and JKP evidence suggests the haircut may be smaller in the Nordics, but sample noise there is larger because there are fewer stocks. Then subtract realistic costs. Only low-turnover themes (value, quality, low-risk filter; momentum with bands) are likely to stay positive net. Treat thesis-level numbers such as 29.4%/yr net as optimistic in-sample artefacts.
- **Combination design.**
  - Build 3–4 theme composites: value (EBIT/EV, E/P, FCF/P, B/P with current prices), quality (GP/A or ROIC, F-score, low accruals, low net issuance), momentum (12-1, volatility-scaled), and a low-IVOL penalty.
  - Within each theme, use equal-weighted rank or z-score averages, in the spirit of JKP's theme clustering and the Fisher-Shah-Titman average rank.
  - Combine themes with fixed, roughly equal weights rather than optimised weights. Optimised weights overfit when there are only 150–300 investable Oslo stocks or 300–700 Stockholm stocks; these counts are approximate inferences, not sourced.
- **Signal half-lives (inference, no direct Nordic estimate found).**
  - Value and quality: re-rank semi-annually or annually; the evidence holds over 12–36 months.
  - Momentum: re-rank monthly to quarterly; Rouwenhorst finds continuation lasts about one year.
  - Low-risk: re-rank quarterly; the effect holds up to 12 months.
  - Events (buybacks, PEAD): decay the weight to zero over 3–12 months.
- **Governance of the app's learning loop.**
  - Pre-register each signal and its weights, and evaluate on logged out-of-sample predictions.
  - Require roughly t > 3 (Harvey-Liu-Zhu) or a Bayesian posterior before upweighting a new home-grown signal.
  - Track per-theme realised information coefficients and shrink weights toward the theme prior (JKP-style) rather than chasing the best recent performer.

### Gaps
- No Nordic-specific estimate of post-publication decay or of signal half-lives was found. Jacobs & Müller's country-level results for Norway and Sweden specifically could not be checked (full paper blocked).
- JKP's paper-level replication rates (for example, the share of US factors replicating, and how the international replication rate compares) were not retrieved beyond the abstract.

## 5. Free (or low-cost) datasets for backtesting Nordic signals: contents and limitations

### Takeaway
For **factor-level** testing, use **Ødegaard's free OSE factors** (Norway, monthly since 1981, including liquidity), **AQR's QMJ and BAB country series** (Denmark, Finland, Norway and Sweden columns), **JKP's country-level factor files** (153 factors, 13 themes, ISO-3 country files, ew/vw/vw_cap weights), and **French's Norway and Sweden value-sorted country portfolios** (from 1975). For **stock-level** signal testing, no free survivorship-free, point-in-time Nordic dataset was found. Börsdata (paid, Pro+ for the API) offers up to 20 years of Nordic prices and fundamentals, but its documentation does not address delisted firms or point-in-time integrity. FinBas/Serrano (SHoF) and TITLON (Norway) are academic-access only. Free regulator registers cover short positions.

#### Kenneth French data library (free)
- International country portfolios formed on **B/M, E/P, CE/P and D/P** include **Norway and Sweden from 1975**, monthly through Dec 2025. They are formed each December, top 30% (value) vs bottom 30% (growth), and value-weighted over the next 12 months. [search excerpt] — [French: international index portfolios](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/Data_Library/int_index_port_formed.html)
- European regional factors (FF3, FF5, momentum) exist for "Europe". Whether the Nordic countries are in the Europe aggregate, and whether Denmark and Finland have country portfolios, was not verified here (see Gaps). — [French data library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html)

#### Swedish House of Finance (SHoF) National Research Data Center (academic access)
- Provides **FinBas** (Swedish stock-market time series: prices, returns, market cap), **Serrano** (Swedish firm accounting data), **Swedish Fama-French factors**, NASDAQ HFT data, Nordic Compass ESG and Morningstar fund data, aimed at the academic community. — [dataportals-registry record](https://github.com/datenoio/dataportals-registry); [PTrees README](https://github.com/michbru/PTrees)
- An AER replication cites "FinBas at Swedish House of Finance Research Data Center, **1983–2016**". — [Crossref reference copy](https://github.com/ledwindra/replication-code-economics)

#### Börsdata (paid; the de facto retail Nordic fundamentals source)
- Covers Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Finland (Nordic, "Pro"), with global coverage for Pro+ since September 2022. Provides **up to 20 years** of end-of-day prices, year, quarter and R12 reports, and KPIs, plus dividend and report calendars. **Insider, short-position and buyback holdings are Pro+ only.** — [Börsdata API README](https://github.com/Borsdata-Sweden/API); [wiki](https://github.com/Borsdata-Sweden/API/wiki)
- **REST API access requires Pro+ from 1 February 2025.** Rate limit is 100 calls per 10 seconds, with fewer than 10,000 per day recommended. Data updates after about 20:00 UTC. There is no intraday data, and global indices and currencies are not included. — [README](https://github.com/Borsdata-Sweden/API); [wiki](https://github.com/Borsdata-Sweden/API/wiki)
- The documentation **does not say** whether delisted instruments are available or whether KPI history is point-in-time (as originally reported) rather than restated. — [README](https://github.com/Borsdata-Sweden/API)

#### Regulator short-position registers (free)
- Sweden (FI Blankningsregistret) publishes daily, normally shortly after 15:30 ([finsyn/blankningsregistret-js](https://github.com/finsyn/blankningsregistret-js); register URLs listed in [boeriksson/candlechart NEWS_SOURCES.md](https://github.com/boeriksson/candlechart/blob/eab470e9fd7c5645e27497c06c388a427aafd1bd/NEWS_SOURCES.md)). Norway (Finanstilsynet SSR) offers an open JSON API at `ssr.finanstilsynet.no/api/v2/instruments/` ([samuelsmb/SSR](https://github.com/samuelsmb/SSR)).

### Inferences
- **Recommended stack.**
  - Use JKP country files (`nor`, `swe`, `dnk`, `fin`; `vw_cap` and `ew`) and AQR QMJ and BAB country columns to estimate realistic theme-level premia, volatility and decay per Nordic country. These are free and survivorship-handled at the factor level.
  - Use Ødegaard's PR1YR, LIQ, SMB and HML as an independent Norwegian cross-check.
  - Use French's Norway and Sweden value portfolios for a long (1975+) value history.
  - Use Börsdata for live stock-level scoring.
  - Do **not** treat Börsdata-only backtests as unbiased until delisted coverage and point-in-time behaviour are confirmed with Börsdata.
  - Mitigate look-ahead bias by lagging reported fundamentals by at least one report-date offset, for example using the report calendar.
- **Survivorship mitigation if Börsdata lacks delisted firms.** Log the full investable universe daily from now on, which builds the app's own survivorship-free history. Validate stock-level backtests against JKP/AQR factor-level results: if a home-grown value backtest beats the JKP Norwegian value theme by a large margin, suspect bias.
- JKP and AQR returns are in **USD** (JKP explicitly says excess USD returns), so convert or compare in local currency carefully when calibrating NOK or SEK expectations.

### Gaps
- **Nasdaq Nordic** historical price downloads (nasdaqomxnordic.com) and **Euronext/Oslo** historical downloads were not checked: no history-length limits, delisted availability, terms of use or bulk-download feasibility could be confirmed.
- French's library: country composition of the "Europe" factors, availability of Denmark and Finland country portfolios, and the current data vendor were not checked.
- Start dates of AQR's Nordic country columns in the *current* files (the BAB paper sample started in 1989) were not checked.
- Börsdata delisted-company coverage and point-in-time KPI behaviour were not confirmed; ask Börsdata support.
- Whether Ødegaard's site still offers daily factor files and liquidity-sorted portfolios, and the current end date of his series, was not confirmed (site blocked).
- No free stock-level, survivorship-free, point-in-time fundamentals source for Norway or Sweden was found.
